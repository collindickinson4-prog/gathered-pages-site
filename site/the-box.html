What’s in the box

One kit per woman: a book, a journal, a pen, a women-owned business card, a letter and the guide that runs the conversation. Ten kits nest in a kraft carton and go to one facilitator.

One kit, one woman

We designed a single kit sized for one person rather than a group box. Ten of them nest inside a plain kraft carton for a facilitator, and the same kit ships on its own when it needs to.

Everything is paper: kraft carton, paper crinkle, paper tape, jute twine, a sticker seal to break. No plastic wrap, no bubble, no gloss. It is cheaper, it is recyclable, and it is honest about what this organization is.

More information: the group reads at whatever pace suits them. Our guide is written in sections so a group that gets halfway still has a real conversation.

More information: the reflection prompts in the guide are written for these pages. Nobody ever has to read theirs out loud.

More information: a small thing, deliberately. A pen that is nicer than it needs to be is the part people notice.

More information: the card is reprinted whenever the makers change, so it always matches what is actually in the box. Every business on it is one we have paid.

More information: it is not a flyer and it does not ask for anything. It says she belongs here, which is the whole point of the box.

More information: one printed Facilitator Welcome card sits on top of the carton explaining how to hand the kits out. That is the entire onboarding.

The order she opens it in

The sequence is the design. It is what turns a box of things into someone having thought about you.

  1. The welcome letter, sitting on top.
  2. The book, banded so opening it feels deliberate.
  3. The journal and pen, tied together as a set.
  4. The women-owned business card, tucked alongside.
  5. A small card with the first meeting details.
